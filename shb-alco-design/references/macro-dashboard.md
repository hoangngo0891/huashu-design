# Macro & Market ALCO Dashboard · v1

## Purpose

Compress the macro/market environment into a decision-useful dashboard for ALCO. The objective is not to reproduce a research report, but to surface the small number of developments that matter for rates, liquidity, FX, credit conditions, and balance-sheet positioning.

## Default structure

Use four layers:

1. **Growth / inflation** — GDP, industrial activity, retail, CPI/core CPI, trade, public investment when relevant.
2. **Money / credit / liquidity** — credit growth, deposit growth, OMO, State Treasury deposits, interbank rates, government bond yields.
3. **FX / external** — USD/VND, DXY, reserves/policy intervention when available, FDI/FII/trade/BOP signals.
4. **Global policy / markets** — Fed/ECB/BOJ/BOE, UST curve, oil/gold/equities only when they materially affect Vietnam or SHB conditions.

## Dashboard principle

Prefer a compact table or tile matrix with trend/signal over a collage of charts.

Each indicator should ideally show:
- latest value;
- change vs prior period / YTD where useful;
- direction or regime;
- one short interpretation.

Do not add a generic `context` column if the same information can be expressed through a concise signal or headline.

## Visual patterns

### Pattern A — Macro pulse matrix
Rows = themes, columns = latest / change / signal / interpretation. Best for appendix or broad overview.

### Pattern B — Four-quadrant dashboard
Growth & inflation / liquidity & rates / FX & external / global markets. Best for executive overview.

### Pattern C — Headline + key charts
Use 2–3 headline statements and 3–4 charts only when specific time-series inflections are central to the story.

## Signal discipline

Signals should describe regime, not pretend to be forecasts. Examples:
- `Cooling`
- `Accelerating`
- `Stable`
- `Tightening`
- `Easing`
- `FX pressure elevated`
- `Liquidity supported`

Use stronger labels such as `Stress` only with clear evidence.

## Executive storyline

The slide should synthesize rather than list. Typical synthesis dimensions:
- growth momentum vs inflation pressure;
- credit demand vs funding availability;
- system liquidity vs TT1 funding pressure;
- FX pressure vs interest-rate flexibility;
- global easing/tightening vs domestic room for policy action.

## Data freshness

- Show the release period/date for major Vietnam macro data.
- Distinguish monthly data, YTD data, period-end market data, and quarterly BOP data.
- Do not combine stale macro releases with live market levels without visible timing labels.

## Design rules

- Tables are preferred when many macro indicators must coexist.
- Charts are reserved for trends, inflections, or divergences.
- Keep decimals limited to analytical usefulness.
- Use arrows sparingly; direction should refer to a defined comparison period.
- Use one concise executive message at the top, supported by 2–4 highlighted indicators.
- Market color should communicate meaning consistently rather than replicate vendor-screen conventions.

## Avoid

- news ticker aesthetics;
- repeating the same headline across multiple sections;
- overloading with global assets that have no ALCO relevance;
- unlabeled comparison periods;
- treating correlation as causation;
- creating a standalone SHB implication box when the user wants implications integrated into the narrative rather than separated.
