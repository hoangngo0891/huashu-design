# Design Principles · v2

## Principle 1 — Start from the communication problem
Do not begin with a layout. Begin with the question the audience must understand or decide.

## Principle 2 — One primary message, multiple supporting layers
Every page/slide should have a clear first-read conclusion. Supporting visuals should reinforce it, not compete with it.

## Principle 3 — Design from evidence
The strongest visual emphasis should follow the strongest evidence. Do not make a decorative element look more important than the data.

## Principle 4 — Adapt to format and audience
A board slide, technical appendix, research note, executive dashboard, and process map require different density and visual behavior. Adapt rather than force one template.

## Principle 5 — Existing context first
When source material exists, learn from it before redesigning. Preserve useful institutional conventions and improve only where they reduce clarity or quality.

## Principle 6 — Precision before decoration
For financial and management communication, exact values, comparison basis, timing, and definitions matter more than visual novelty.

## Principle 7 — Use whitespace as structure
Whitespace should separate levels of meaning and reduce cognitive load. It is not empty space to be filled.

## Principle 8 — Create visual hierarchy deliberately
Use size, weight, placement, spacing, contrast, and alignment to make the intended reading order obvious.

## Principle 9 — Minimize unnecessary visual grammar
Do not add icons, cards, colors, borders, labels, or illustrations unless they improve recognition, grouping, comparison, or emphasis.

## Principle 10 — Reuse patterns, do not worship them
A proven pattern is useful when it fits the problem. If it does not fit, create a better structure from first principles.

## Principle 11 — Preserve editability
Where practical, keep text, charts, tables, and shapes editable so the deliverable can be maintained by the business team.

## Principle 12 — Institutional, not generic AI
Outputs should look intentionally authored for SHB and for the specific subject matter. Avoid generic consulting aesthetics and generic AI wording.
