# Charts & Tables · v2

## General rule
Choose the visual form from the analytical task, not from habit.

## Tables
Use when exact values, definitions, or side-by-side comparison matter.
- Keep units in headers where possible.
- Use light separators and restrained highlighting.
- Prefer explicit variance columns such as YoY, YTD, vs plan, MoM, QoQ when comparison matters.
- Do not replace quantitative comparison with decorative arrows.

## Bars
Use for magnitude, peer comparison, ranking, and progress-to-target.
- Use a common scale for comparable values.
- Add target/reference markers when decision-relevant.

## Lines
Use for time series, divergence, inflection, and regime change.
- Label timing clearly.
- Highlight only analytically important points.

## Waterfall / bridge
Use for movement decomposition where components add to a total change.

## Flow diagrams
Use for transmission, source/use, and process relationships.
- Arrow thickness may imply magnitude only when quantities are comparable.
- Do not imply causal or accounting identities unsupported by data.

## Rings / donuts
Use sparingly for simple composition or progress. Avoid ring overload.

## Financial-data discipline
Always distinguish:
- stock vs flow;
- period-end vs average;
- YTD vs YoY vs MoM vs QoQ;
- actual vs plan vs forecast;
- reported vs estimated data.

## Avoid
- 3D charts;
- decorative gauges;
- unlabeled axes or comparison bases;
- excessive data labels;
- color without analytical meaning.
